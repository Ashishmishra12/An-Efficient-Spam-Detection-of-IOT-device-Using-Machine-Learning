Correlation Matrix Explanation

This heatmap visualizes how different features (e.g., message length, TF-IDF score, special character count) relate to one another in the spam detection dataset. Each cell shows a numerical correlation value between -1 and +1.

  • +1: Perfect positive correlation – both values increase together.
  • -1: Perfect negative correlation – one increases while the other decreases.
  • 0: No linear relationship.

Color Legend: Dark red indicates strong positive correlation, dark blue indicates strong negative correlation, and lighter shades show weaker relationships.

Weather Feature Correlation

This matrix shows the interrelation between weather features such as temperature, humidity, dew point, and visibility. Like the previous heatmap, it uses correlation coefficients to measure how strongly two features move together.

  • High values (e.g., 0.95) indicate a strong relationship (e.g., temperature vs. apparent temperature).
  • Low or negative values indicate weak or inverse relationships (e.g., humidity vs. visibility).

Color Legend: Deep red signifies strong positive correlation, while deep blue indicates strong negative correlation. White or light colors reflect weak or no correlation.

Efficient Spam Detection

This pie chart illustrates the distribution of spam and non-spam messages in the dataset. The chart shows how much of the dataset is categorized as spam and how much is categorized as non-spam.

  • Spam: Represents the proportion of messages identified as spam.
  • No Spam: Represents the proportion of messages that are not identified as spam.

Color Legend: The pie chart displays the proportions in different colors to distinguish spam from non-spam messages.
